# BUNA ATELIER — Premium Ethiopian Coffee House Website
## $10K-Level Website Design Specification + Claude Code Master Prompt

> **Project type:** Premium Ethiopian coffee house digital experience  
> **Concept brand:** BUNA ATELIER — ቡና አትሌየር  
> **Location:** Addis Ababa, Ethiopia  
> **Website goal:** Create a cinematic, culturally grounded, modern coffee-house website that feels like a premium hospitality brand rather than a generic café template.  
> **Important:** BUNA ATELIER is a fictional concept brand created for a portfolio/demo project.

---

# 1. THE BIG IDEA

This website should not look like:

- a normal restaurant template
- a generic coffee shop landing page
- a Bootstrap/Tailwind demo
- a developer portfolio pretending to be a café
- a page full of random Ethiopian patterns
- a website that simply puts coffee photos behind large text

It should feel like a **digital extension of the physical coffee experience**.

The central concept is:

> **BUNA IS A MOMENT, NOT JUST A DRINK.**

The experience should move the visitor through:

**ORIGIN → ROAST → RITUAL → CUP → COMMUNITY → VISIT**

The website should tell a visual story before it asks the visitor to do anything.

# 3. DESIGN RESEARCH INSIGHTS

Research into Addis Ababa's coffee scene shows a strong coexistence between traditional buna culture and modern specialty coffee.

Traditional coffee ceremony commonly involves:

- roasting green coffee beans
- grinding the beans
- brewing in a jebena
- serving in small cups
- incense/aroma
- social gathering
- multiple rounds of coffee

The ceremony is fundamentally social and hospitality-oriented rather than simply a beverage transaction.

The modern Addis specialty scene also includes:

- single-origin coffee
- pour-over
- espresso
- roasting
- coffee education
- coffee tasting
- modern café interiors
- creative spaces
- food pairings

The website should combine these two worlds rather than choosing only one.

Research references used for this concept include Ethiopian tourism material, current Addis coffee directories, and existing Ethiopian specialty-coffee websites. Examples include Adorsi Coffee, GOJEB Coffee, Green Gold, and Addis specialty-coffee directories.

The concept should NOT copy their layouts, wording, branding, photographs, or identity.

Useful research observations:

1. Ethiopian coffee culture has strong ritual and hospitality dimensions.
2. Addis has both traditional buna houses and contemporary specialty cafés.
3. Modern coffee brands increasingly communicate origin, roasting, tasting, events, and space.
4. A premium café website benefits from showing the physical experience, not just menu items.
5. Addis is visually rich enough to support a strong editorial design language.
